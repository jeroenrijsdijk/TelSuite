What is telonline.org?

Traffic counting online — from fieldwork to a map report

telonline.org is a set of online browser tools for traffic counting: cars, bicycles, pedestrians, parked vehicles and parking capacity. Everything runs in your own browser. There is nothing to install and no account to create. The apps use GPS and fit your observations onto the OpenStreetMap network, so every count becomes a usable dataset tied to a place.

The aim is an easy counting app in your pocket. A lot of metadata is collected automatically, and the processing afterwards is made as simple as possible. The tools are just as suitable for regular, structured counts.

Most screens are in Dutch. The front page and the start screen of Pocket also come in English, and so does the reference for the ZIP files.

Who is it for?

  • Traffic engineers at municipalities, provinces and consultancies who want to carry out field counts quickly and consistently, without installing software.
  • Students and researchers collecting traffic or mobility data for a study or a thesis.
  • Anyone who is curious how busy a street, a shopping area or a parking zone really is. The tools are free to use.

In short: count → ZIP → reconstruct → map.

  • Count. Pick an app, press Start, tap what you see. GPS runs along and snaps your taps to the OpenStreetMap network. At the end you get one ZIP file. Don't unzip it.
  • Post-process. Drop that ZIP into the reconstruction. It looks at your whole route at once and puts every tap on the right street after all, with the side of the road and the walking direction. Your count itself does not change; only the positions get better.
  • View. Drop one or more ZIPs into Telrapport, the report tool: how busy each road section is, a day×hour weekly profile, filters on time and session. Once the map looks the way you want, save it as a collection ZIP and you can get back to it later in one step.

No installation, no account, no upload: everything runs in your own browser. Prefer GIS? The ZIPs load straight into QGIS, and Telrapport saves the map as a GeoPackage with a ready-made QGIS project.

How it works: three steps

The front page groups the tools in three. Count traffic and Count parking are the field apps of step 1. At your desk holds what you do before and after: planning, reconstruction (step 2), the report (step 3), pedestrians per hour in a shopping street, and the archive: all your counts on a map, and your ZIPs sorted.

Before you go: planning (optional). In Telplanning you pick the area and the streets you want to count. The plan goes to your phone as a link. Open it in Pocket, car parking or bicycle parking: the streets appear on the map, and the app fetches the road network for the whole area straight away, so you don't have to wait for it while you walk. Step 1: Count

Go to telonline.org and pick a field app that fits what you want to know.

GPS snaps every count to the OpenStreetMap road network. At the end of the session the app exports the result as one .zip file with the counts and the GPS route. Keep the ZIPs intact; there is no need to unzip them. If you organise the ZIP files in a logical way, finding things later is much easier. The Archief (archive, in Dutch) helps. It shows all your counts on a map and in a table: where and when, what is a duplicate and what has no reconstruction yet. And it sorts your ZIPs: a folder per type of count, and originals that have a reconstruction in a subfolder. The maps and reports themselves you make in the report tool. Every count gets a unique three-letter code.

Step 2: Post-process the ZIP (recommended)

While you count, the app knows roughly where you are, but not always exactly. GPS is a few metres off by nature, and at a junction or between tall buildings a tap can land on the wrong street.

The reconstruction looks at your whole walk afterwards, in one go. Because it knows the route as a whole, it can tell much better which street you actually walked, and it puts every tap in the right place after all. It also adds which side of the road you counted and which way you walked. If a tap still ends up in the wrong place, you correct it yourself with a click on the map.

The result is a cleaner map: the counts sit on the right street, and your report is more accurate. Your count itself does not change: the numbers stay exactly what you counted, only the positions get more precise.

Step 3: Present it in Telrapport

Drop one or more ZIPs into Telrapport for a combined map of the counts: how busy each road section is, a day×hour weekly profile that shows when it is busy week after week, intensity classes, and filters on time and type of session. This is where the first two steps lead: from loose fieldwork to one readable picture.

Once the map looks the way you want (a few counts side by side, the right layers on, the right view) you save it as a collection ZIP. That is one file with all the counts inside, plus how the map looked. Drop it back into Telrapport later and you are back where you were in one step, including colours, visibility and zoom level. Corrections you made by hand come along. Do keep your own count ZIPs next to it: the collection is your working map, not your archive.

Prefer GIS software? The ZIPs also load straight into QGIS, with a layer for each type of count. The Python loader script is telonline_qgis_loader.py. If you mainly want a neat map, save the map as you see it in Telrapport as a GeoPackage. That is one file with the observations, the cluster circles, the road sections, the walked routes and the parking capacity, with Telrapport's colours built in. It also holds a QGIS project: open it with Project → Open From → GeoPackage and the map is ready, on a PDOK base map (the Netherlands) and with an A4 print layout for a PDF. Choose the file name when you save, and don't rename the file afterwards, or the project can no longer find its layers. More in the manual (in Dutch).
